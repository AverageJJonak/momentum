## Speed control: the snowball

Each segment ridden by a player who perfect-hops every flat with headroom (×1.18 a landing, fading past 18 m/s), slides every downhill, presses at every landing and keeps the wall jump's gain; capped at 40 m/s, the fastest the recordings show. Twice: FROM THE PAD (run speed at the segment's start) and CARRIED (with what the segment before handed on). At every gap the rider sheds to the most speed the move still lands at, so what it hands on is what a player who never falls can build. A ground turn of 60° or more, a settle corner, a kink pad, a step and a climb shed to run speed; a roof under 1.95 m is a slide (−0.6 m/s a metre); a roof under 3.3 m stops the hops but keeps the speed (a hop that bonks under a low roof is not modelled). Every gap of the line, and every skip between two floors of it, is tried at the speed the rider arrives with: a plain jump that lands clean at 1.4× the sized speed or more is TRIVIAL. The gap's own move is tried at every speed up from the sized one until a lintel is hit or no floor is under the landing: 'lands up to'. A straight is 20 m or more of flat with headroom; a bend (floors named Bend…, fifteen degrees a facet) is gathered like a flat, so what a hop chain steered round it builds is carried, but a bend is not reported as a straight.

## Walls that lift

Every wall a run can be entered on from a floor at its foot, taken the worst way: jumped at (9 m/s up at the touch). A wall's top ends the run early; a ceiling over the run stops the rise. REACHES lists every surface at least a body above the floor and no higher than the lift within 32 m of the wall.
